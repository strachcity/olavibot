# Everyday nutrition for a training adult: what the evidence says

Question: how much protein and carbohydrate does Jack's training call for, how should it be
timed, and how do you avoid gaining fat without under-fuelling? This covers daily eating, not
race fuelling (backyard fuelling is already solved, see `wiki/training-model.md`).

## Findings

1. **Joint position statement, the main source.** Thomas, Erdman & Burke 2016, *Nutrition and
   Athletic Performance*. Joint position of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, Dietitians
   of Canada and the ACSM. *J Acad Nutr Diet* 116(3):501–528, doi:10.1016/j.jand.2015.12.006;
   also published in *Med Sci Sports Exerc*. Evidence review March 2006 → November 2014. Full
   text read: https://www.dietitians.ca/DietitiansOfCanada/media/Documents/Resources/noap-position-paper.pdf

   a. **Daily carbohydrate scales with training load** (g per kg bodyweight per day):

   | Load | Example | Target |
   |---|---|---|
   | Light | Low intensity or skill-based | 3–5 |
   | Moderate | Moderate exercise programme, ~1 h/day | 5–7 |
   | High | Endurance programme, 1–3 h/day moderate to high intensity | 6–10 |
   | Very high | >4–5 h/day moderate to high intensity | 8–12 |

   Carbs can be timed before, during or after a specific session for high availability there.
   Otherwise, if total needs are met, the pattern over the day "may simply be guided by
   convenience and individual choice." Choose nutrient-rich carbohydrate sources.

   b. **Pre-exercise:** 1–4 g/kg in the 1–4 h before, for events or key sessions over 60 minutes.

   c. **Protein:** generally 1.2–2.0 g/kg/day. Higher, for short periods, during intensified
   training or when cutting energy intake. Spread moderate amounts of high-quality protein
   across the day: ~0.3 g/kg (15–25 g) after key sessions and every 3–5 hours over several
   meals. Doses above ~40 g not yet shown to add to muscle protein synthesis. Needs vary with
   training status, session type, carbohydrate availability and, "most importantly", energy
   availability.

   d. **Energy availability (EA)** = intake minus exercise expenditure, per kg fat-free mass.
   ~45 kcal/kg FFM/day was associated with energy balance and good health. Chronic intake below
   ~30 was associated with impaired body functions. The concept was first studied in women.

   e. **Losing body fat while training:** if loss is wanted, schedule it in the base phase or
   well away from competition. Use a slight deficit for a slow loss, and raise protein. One
   2-week study in athletes found 2.3 g/kg kept muscle better than 1.0 g/kg on a restricted
   diet. FFM and performance may be better kept when loss stays under 1% of bodyweight a week.
   The practical approach for most athletes is to cut intake by ~250–500 kcal/day from
   periodised needs while keeping or slightly raising expenditure, over roughly 3–6 weeks.

   f. **Alcohol:** 7 kcal/g; it suppresses fat oxidation, increases unplanned eating and "may
   compromise the achievement of body composition goals". The research has design problems
   that limit direct translation to athletes.

2. **Protein and resistance training.** Morton et al. 2018, *Br J Sports Med* 52(6):376–384.
   Systematic review, meta-analysis and meta-regression: 49 RCTs, 1,863 participants,
   resistance training of 6 weeks or more. Protein supplementation increased gains in strength
   and fat-free mass. Above a total intake of ~1.62 g/kg/day, no further fat-free-mass gain.
   The benefit falls with age and rises with training experience. Abstract only:
   https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/items/8dea98a4-4e37-4c60-be43-4b6ece0bd9a5
   (a secondary summary says the authors suggested 2.2 g/kg as a prudent upper target because
   the confidence interval was wide; not checked against the paper).

3. **Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs).** Mountjoy et al. 2023, IOC consensus
   statement, *Br J Sports Med* 57(17):1073–1097. Low energy availability runs on a spectrum
   from adaptable (mild or brief, benign) to problematic (prolonged or severe). REDs is the
   syndrome that follows problematic exposure, in men as well as women; the 2023 update
   reports progress in knowledge of REDs in male athletes. Includes a clinical assessment tool
   (REDs CAT2). Read via secondary summaries only:
   https://humanperformancealliance.org/news/2023-international-olympic-committee-consensus-statement-on-reds/

4. **Day-to-day bodyweight is noisy.** Search results (popular health sites, not primary
   studies) consistently describe day-to-day swings of ~1–2 kg as normal, driven by water,
   gut contents, sodium and glycogen. Glycogen is stored with roughly 3 g of water per gram.
   The standard advice is to weigh under the same conditions and compare weekly averages. No
   primary study for the size of the daily swing was found in this pass.

## Not covered
- Micronutrients (iron, vitamin D, calcium) beyond "nutrient-rich sources".
- Supplements, caffeine, hydration targets, race and in-session fuelling.
- Measuring fat-free mass. Without it, EA can't be calculated for Jack, only reasoned about.
