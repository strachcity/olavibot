# Warm-up (ramp) sets before resistance training — what the evidence says

Question: what is the evidence behind ramp-up sets before working sets, and how should they
be loaded?

## Findings

1. **Warming up in general improves performance.** Fradkin, Zazryn & Smoliga 2010,
   *J Strength Cond Res* 24(1):140–148, systematic review with meta-analysis, 32 studies
   (quality 6.5–9/10). Warm-up improved performance in 79% of the criteria examined.
   Covers physical performance broadly, not resistance training specifically.
   https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/fulltext/2010/01000/effects_of_warming_up_on_physical_performance__a.21.aspx

2. **Research on resistance-training warm-ups is thin and mixed.** A systematic review in
   *Motricidade* (2021, "The effect of warm-up in resistance training and strength
   performance") calls the scarcity of research "notorious". Results on general plus specific
   warm-up weren't consistent: some studies found a specific warm-up no better than none, and
   others found a specific warm-up alone best for maximal strength.
   https://revistas.rcaap.pt/motricidade/article/view/21143

3. **Specific warm-ups raise 1RM and bar speed.** A 2025 scoping review, *J Sci Sport
   Exercise* ("Acute Effects of Resistance Training Warm-Up and Re-Warm-Up on Dynamic Strength
   Performance"), 19 studies: resistance-training warm-ups increase leg press and squat 1RM,
   and progressive-intensity warm-ups increase lifting velocity.
   https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42978-025-00361-9

4. **No effect on reps to failure in one trial.** Ribeiro et al. 2014, *Percept Mot Skills*,
   n=15 men, 4 sets to failure at 80% 1RM (bench, squat, curl) under control, specific,
   aerobic and combined warm-ups. No significant difference in total reps or fatigue index.
   https://doi.org/10.2466/25.29.pms.119c17z7

5. **A heavier final warm-up set helped in two small trials.**
   - Ribeiro et al. 2020, *IJERPH* 17(18):6882, n=40 resistance-trained men, 3×6 @80% 1RM
     after one of three warm-ups: 6 @80% of training load, 6 @40%, or both. Squat bar speed
     was higher after the 80% warm-up than the 40% one (ES 0.5–0.8).
     https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17186882
   - 2024 study, *J Bodyw Mov Ther* ("High-load and low-volume warm-up increases performance
     in a resistance training session"), n=15 trained men. A warm-up at 80% of 10RM × 5 reps
     gave more total training volume than 60% × 10 or 40% × 15. The authors recommend a
     warm-up at ~80% of the starting load for each exercise.
     https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1360859224004017

6. **Not replicated in the most recent trial.** 2025, ScienceDirect article S2666337625000988, journal not confirmed
   (preprint: SportRxiv 559), "Warming up to improved
   performance?", resistance-trained individuals. Changing the specific warm-up's number of
   sets or load "neither appreciably enhanced nor impaired" session performance.
   https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666337625000988

7. **Warm-up and injury in resistance training: no direct evidence.** Sources found say no
   studies test warm-ups against injury in resistance training specifically. The broader
   sport RCT review (DARE-appraised) found insufficient evidence to endorse or drop routine
   warm-ups for injury, with the weight of evidence leaning towards reduced risk.
   https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK72912/

## Read-across (Claude)

- Warming up is worth doing; that part is solid.
- How ramp sets should be structured rests on a handful of small, short-term trials in young
  trained men, with mixed results. The most consistent signal: finish the ramp with a low-rep
  set near the working load (~80% of it), and don't pile up reps on the way.
- Practical conventions (e.g. bar × 8–10 → ~50% × 5 → ~70% × 3 → ~85–90% × 1–2) are
  practitioner heuristics consistent with that signal, not tested prescriptions.
- The case for ramp sets as injury prevention in lifting is mechanistic reasoning, not data.
