## Diagnosis matters
- Mid-portion (2–6cm above heel) tolerates full dorsiflexion loading; insertional does not —
  heel drops off a step can aggravate it. **W**
- Recurrent symptoms over 9+ months = persistent presentation. Expect months, not weeks.
  Pain tracks poorly with tissue state; judge by function and load tolerance. **W**
  *Applying this to Jack: the report assumed a 9+ month chronic picture from partial data.
  Fuller Strava data plus his own account since established this as a single recent episode
  (May–Aug 2026), not a multi-year condition — see `athlete.md`. The general evidence above
  still holds; it just may not describe his case. Don't import "expect months, not weeks"
  without checking which situation actually applies.*
- Differentials worth ruling out in person: Haglund's/retrocalcaneal bursitis, plantaris,
  posterior tibialis, bone stress. **W**

## Loading
- Progressive loading is first-line. No protocol clearly beats another. Give exercise alone
  ≥3 months before considering adjuncts. **W** (van der Vlist 2021, network meta-analysis)
- **Heavy slow resistance** (Beyer 2015): 3 exercises (seated bent-knee, standing
  straight-knee, leg-press calf), 3×/wk, 3s up/3s down. 15RM → 12RM → 10RM → 8RM → 6RM over
  12 weeks. Same outcomes as eccentric, better compliance (92% vs 78%), ~41 min/wk. **W**
- Eccentric (Alfredson): 3×15 ×2 positions, twice daily, 12 weeks. Works; heavy time cost. **W**
- Isometrics for pain relief: contested. Original evidence tiny (n=6, patellar tendon, not
  even Achilles); a later quasi-RCT specifically in Achilles tendinopathy found no reliable
  immediate analgesia. Fine as a low-irritability start, not a pain switch. **W** (for the
  critique)
- Timeline: meaningful change from ~2 weeks (mostly neural), ~21-point VISA-A gain at 12
  weeks. **W**

## Pain-monitoring model (Silbernagel 2007) **W**
- Pain during/after loading acceptable up to ~5/10
- Settles to baseline by next morning
- Not climbing week to week
- Morning first-steps stiffness is the key signal
- Runners who kept running under this model did as well as those who rested

## Calf capacity
- Achilles loads ~6–12× bodyweight in running (method-dependent) ★. Komi's in-vivo figure of
  ~9kN / ~12.5× bodyweight is a single-subject maximal value; neuromusculoskeletal modelling
  gives lower means of ~2–4× BW. Treat the range as method-dependent, not a fixed number.
  Soleus is the biggest contributor → bent-knee work matters as much as straight-knee. **W**
- Single-leg heel raise to fatigue: judge on **work and height**, not reps alone — reps
  recover faster than work. Target limb symmetry ≥80–90%. **W**
- Norms are protocol-dependent. Use left-vs-right symmetry as the main gauge. **I**

## Load management
- The 10% rule has no evidence behind it. **W**
- Acute:chronic workload ratio is heavily criticised (ratio artefacts, arbitrary thresholds,
  no intervention evidence). Don't use it as a target. **W**
- Strongest injury risk factor: previous injury. **W**
- Single-session spikes (one run much longer than recent longest) matter more than gradual
  weekly build. **W** ★ (~930-runner cohort). *Independently corroborated in Jack's own data —
  see `strava-history.md`: the 127km backyard was ×2.8 his 28-day longest, Sydney was ×5.7.*
- Higher chronic load is protective. Build capacity, don't stay cautious forever. **W**
- Strength training cut overuse injuries ~47% and total injuries to under a third; stretching
  did nothing (Lauersen 2014). Dose-response exists. **W**

## Concurrent training
- Interference is small at recreational volumes. **W**
- Separate heavy legs and hard running by ~6h or different days. Run first if running
  quality matters most that day. **W/I**
- Heavy lower-body lifting can dent running economy for ~6–8h afterwards. **I**
