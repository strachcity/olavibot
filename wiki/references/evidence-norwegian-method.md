# Evidence: the Norwegian Method and double threshold training

Compiled from `raw/research/norwegian-method/` — four Marius Bakken articles (2000, 2022,
2025, 2026) and the community-maintained *Norwegian Singles* guide (PDF).
Grades: **W** well-supported · **I** reasonable inference · **H** practitioner heuristic.

**Sourcing caveat.** Overwhelmingly one person's first-person account of his own training and
coaching, not a literature review — Bakken says as much himself. Two citations inside it are
real and checkable (a small 2024 crossover trial; a review Bakken co-authored). Everything
else, including specific lactate figures, is anecdote however precise it sounds.

**The Ingebrigtsen dispute.** Bakken's proof-of-success chain runs mainly through the
Ingebrigtsen family. In 2025 he quotes Gjert Ingebrigtsen's sworn court testimony denying it
outright: *"This system is mine and only mine... no one has influenced it."* Bakken disputes
this back. Neither side is verified here — read every "and that's why they're so good" line
as contested, not settled.

**Population mismatch.** The examples run 150–220 km/week: Bakken, the Ingebrigtsens,
Kristensen. Jack peaks 60–70, currently rebuilding from 15–19. Nothing here should be read as
"do what they do, smaller."

---

## What applies

**The core mechanism reframes the grey-zone problem.** Bakken's "sweet spot" sits at a pace
Western coaching would often call grey-zone. The difference isn't the pace band — it's that
it's run as **controlled reps with jog recoveries, bounded by an effort ceiling**, never as
continuous running at an undefined pace. Effort is checked by breath and a talk test, never by
chasing a heart-rate number, and a rep finishes with enough left for another. **I**

Applied in `training-model.md` §5: the weekly threshold session now states the effort ceiling
explicitly, and names *structure* rather than pace as what separates it from grey-zone running.

**Its progression method matches Phase A.** Change one existing run into one controlled
workout, hold total time flat, don't add a second workout until repeated weeks show full
recovery. Close to what `plan-training` already does; no change needed, just confirmation.

## What doesn't apply

**Double threshold.** Every population it's built for races 5k–10k at elite volume. Its own
source excludes the marathon ("you definitely need more continuous work") and calls it "not a
default solution" for ultras — Jack's two targets. Considered and ruled out; not in the model.
