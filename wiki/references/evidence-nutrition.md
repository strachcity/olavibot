**Sourcing (Oct 2026):** better than passes 1–3, not complete. The 2016 joint position

Scope: daily eating against training. Race fuelling is out of scope; it's already solved.

## Protein
- 1.2–2.0 g/kg/day covers most training. **W** (2016 joint position statement)
- The gain in muscle and strength from extra protein flattens at ~1.6 g/kg/day total, in
  people doing resistance training. **W** (Morton 2018, 49 RCTs)
- Spread it: ~0.3 g/kg (15–25 g) after key sessions and every 3–5 hours over several meals.
  Above ~40 g a dose, no added benefit has been shown. **I** (position statement, mainly lab
  studies of muscle protein synthesis; long-term effects less clear)
- Go higher (towards 2.0+ g/kg) when cutting energy intake, to keep muscle. **I** (one
  2-week study, 2.3 vs 1.0 g/kg)

## Carbohydrate
- Scale it to the day's load, g/kg/day: light 3–5, moderate (~1 h/day) 5–7, endurance
  1–3 h/day 6–10, >4–5 h/day 8–12. **I** (expert consensus bands rather than trial-derived
  numbers)
- For a key session over 60 minutes (the long run), 1–4 g/kg in the 1–4 h before. **I**
- Outside key sessions, timing can follow convenience as long as the daily total is met. **I**
- Choose nutrient-rich sources. **H**

## Avoiding fat gain without under-fuelling
- If fat loss is wanted, the base phase is the time for it, not near a race. **I**
- A slow loss, under 1% of bodyweight a week, keeps more muscle and performance. **I** (one
  cited study)
- The practical method: ~250–500 kcal/day under periodised needs, protein up, training kept,
  over ~3–6 weeks. **H**
- Alcohol adds 7 kcal/g, blunts fat oxidation and raises unplanned eating. **I** (the research
  has design problems)
- Low energy availability runs from adaptable (brief, mild) to problematic (prolonged,
  severe). Problematic exposure causes REDs, in men too. **W** (IOC 2023 consensus, read
  second-hand)
- Energy availability ≥45 kcal/kg fat-free mass/day goes with good health. Chronic intake
  below ~30 impairs body function. **I** (thresholds first studied in women; needs a
  fat-free-mass measure Jack doesn't have)

## Reading bodyweight
- Day-to-day swings of ~1–2 kg are normal (water, gut contents, sodium, glycogen). Compare
  weekly averages on the same scale under the same conditions. **H** (secondary sources only)
- A carb-heavy day or a glycogen top-up can add water weight that isn't fat. **H**
