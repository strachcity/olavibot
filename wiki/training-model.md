# Training Model

The decisions. Not the evidence — that lives in `wiki/references/`. This is what we've chosen
and why, so that session planning doesn't get re-argued every week.

Owner: Jack. Last revised: September 2026.

---

## 1. What we're training for

**The underlying aim: a durable, all-round athlete.** Strong, robust, mobile, fast enough,
aerobically deep, and still enjoying it in five years. Race targets are expressions of that,
not replacements for it.

**Primary target: a 24-hour backyard ultra.** 24 laps of 6.706km, 161km. Best to date:
19 laps / 127km (Mackyard, May 2026).

**Secondary, dormant: a sub-3 marathon.** No race booked; expect 12–16 weeks' notice.
The all-round base is what a sub-3 block would sharpen. See §10.

**Not a goal:** winning, placing, or racing other people.

## 2. Athlete profile

### Benchmarks (official times)
5k 17:55 · 10k 39:35 · HM 1:28 · Marathon 3:11 (Florence 2024). Backyard 101km → ~70km →
127km over three attempts.

The 5k and half both predict a marathon under 3:05, the 5k closer to 2:55. The marathon is
the underperformer: the engine is there, converting it into 42km is not yet. He has run one
genuinely structured marathon block (Florence), and it produced the PB.

### Strengths to protect
- Aerobic durability and appetite for long, steady work.
- Commitment, discipline, planning, willingness to do volume.
- Responds well to strength training — the 2024 knee issue resolved once the gym became
  serious and stretching became regular.
- Already solved backyard fuelling and sleep deprivation.
- Genuine speed for a recreational runner.

### Weak links (weighted, not exclusive)
None of these gets its own programme. Each gets extra weight inside a balanced one.
- **Achilles** — a single episode following the May 2026 backyard, not a historical condition
  `[stated]`. Symptoms mainly 2–6cm above the heel (consistent with mid-portion). Pain-free
  since ~10 Aug 2026. Calf capacity is the presumed gap, but the training history is
  unrecorded rather than known empty — see `athlete.md`.
- **Hip flexors** — failed at hour 19 of the 2026 backyard. Could not lift the leg into
  a running stride. Never directly trained.
- **Grey-zone running** — since 2025 most runs sit at 4:45–5:15/km: too fast to be easy, too
  slow to build anything. ~52–60% of running time since 2025Q1, against 20.5% in the Florence
  quarter `[data: Strava]`. It replaced quality, not easy running — easy has never exceeded a
  third of training time in any year on record, so §5's ~80% easy target has never been met.
- **Movement is almost entirely forward.** Running, squats, deadlifts. Little lateral,
  rotational, reactive or single-leg-balance work.
- **Restraint** — see §3.

### The lap pace experiment
2026 laps were run deliberately slow (7:40–8:25/km) to test whether slower meant less tired.
It left 4–8 minutes of recovery per hour instead of 13–15 at 6:45–7:00/km. Result: clear.
Faster laps are the rest strategy.

## 3. The behavioural pattern

**His biggest training risk isn't laziness. It's enthusiasm.**

Examples: the planned 20–30 minute easy comeback run whose first 3km went at 4:15/km because
he was late (17 Jul 2026, four days after the flare — 5.7km, verified on Strava); squats run to
8 reps at 90kg when 6 were planned and lateral squats jumped 12.5→15kg in the same session
(19 Aug, eleven days out from Sydney); dead hangs added after being told to skip grip work
(23 Aug, eight days out). He is excellent at committing and doing. He is less good at stopping.

*This insight is not ours.* It came from the ChatGPT coaching conversation, 19 Jul 2026 —
"your biggest training risk isn't laziness, it's enthusiasm" — and Jack adopted it. The gym log
then produced three fresh instances inside a single taper week, which is why it sits in
Confirmed rather than Hypotheses `[data: gym log, Aug 2026]`.

So the system builds restraint in structurally rather than relying on willpower:
- **Every session has a stated ceiling**, not just a target. "40 minutes easy, stop at 45."
- **Good-day allowances are pre-agreed.** If a session feels great, the permitted extra is
  specific and small — 4 strides, one more set of an accessory, 5 more minutes easy. Never
  more pace, never more load on an anchor.
- **Reviews ask two questions:** what did you do beyond the plan, and why.
- **Report capacity, not just symptoms.** Not "did it hurt?" but "what can I comfortably do
  this week that I couldn't last week?" — also from the ChatGPT conversation, 19 Jul 2026.

## 4. Principles

These override session-level optimisation.

1. **Adherence beats optimality.** A session he'll do at 80% beats a perfect one he skips.
   If a block stops feeling good, change the block.
2. **Balance before specificity.** Every capacity is covered on a weekly or fortnightly
   rhythm (§6). Weak links get weighted; nothing gets neglected, and nothing dominates.
   Chasing the last thing that broke is how the next thing breaks.
3. **Variation is a requirement.** He responds to novelty and learning. Squat and deadlift
   are fixed anchors. Everything else rotates between blocks, strategically — for a reason,
   never at random.
4. **Every session has a warm-up and a cool-down.** 5–10 minutes each, gym and run.
5. **Progress one variable at a time.** Volume OR intensity, never both in the same fortnight.
6. **Easy must be genuinely easy.** Conversational, RPE ≤4, roughly 5:30–6:00/km or slower.
7. **Morning first-steps stiffness is the governing Achilles signal.**
8. **Feeling good is not a reason to do more.** See §3.

## 5. The base week

Year-round default.

| | |
|---|---|
| Runs | 4–5 |
| Gym | 2 |
| Quality | 1 threshold session |
| Speed | Strides twice a week (4–6 × 20s, relaxed-fast, full recovery) |
| Long run | 1, progressed by duration |
| Cross-training | Optional, counts toward aerobic load |
| Distribution | ~80% easy / 20% harder, by time |

**Threshold.** Controlled reps with a jog recovery, not continuous running: 3×8–10min,
4×8min, 3×15min, off 2min, at an effort just below half-marathon pace. Breathe harder than easy
but hold a short phrase; finish each rep with enough left for one more. Don't chase a pace or
HR number against what breath and legs say — the point is a session he can repeat, not one he
survives. This structure, not just the pace band, is what fixes grey-zone running: the same
effort run continuously and undosed is the grey zone; run as bounded reps, it's a session
`[I]` (`references/evidence-norwegian-method.md`). This shift from short fast intervals to
longer reps is what moved him from 3:24 to 3:11.

**Strides.** Keep speed and running mechanics alive at almost no fatigue cost. After easy runs.
This is also the sanctioned outlet for a good-legs day.

**Long run.** Duration first, pace second. Mixes terrain: hills with deliberate knee drive,
trails for ankle robustness.

**Cross-training.** Cycling (he already cycles to the gym), hiking with hills. Aerobic work
that spares the Achilles and trains hip flexion. Counts as easy volume.

**Occasional speed session.** One short VO2-type session every 2–3 weeks in Phase B, replacing
that week's threshold. Keeps the 5k engine and adds variety. Not in Phase A.

**Not adopting double threshold.** Two threshold sessions a day is built for 150–220km/wk
runners racing 5k–10k. Jack peaks 60–70, currently rebuilding from ~15–19. Neither of his
targets is the event it's built for — its own source excludes the marathon ("you definitely
need more continuous work") and calls it "not a default solution" for ultras. Considered and
ruled out; see `references/evidence-norwegian-method.md`.

## 6. Gym structure

Two sessions a week, 45–75 minutes. Two gyms: fully equipped, and a flat gym with no row or
lat pulldown. Every session plan states which gym.

Evidence basis: `references/evidence-gym-pool.md`. Grades in brackets: **[W]** well-supported,
**[I]** reasonable inference, **[H]** practitioner heuristic.

### The core idea: protect the doses that matter, rotate the rest

Touching every capacity every week sounds balanced, but in 2 × 60 minutes it gives several of
them a dose too small to do anything. So capacities fall into three tiers.

**Tier 1 — every week, real dose.** Best evidence for economy, durability or his known failure
points.

| Capacity | Where | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| Squat (anchor) | Session A | 3–5 × 3–6, ≥80% 1RM in strength blocks **[W]** |
| Deadlift (anchor) | Session B | 3–5 × 3–6, straps on top sets **[W]** |
| Calf — heavy slow | Both | 3–4 × 6–12, bent-knee AND straight-knee **[W]** |
| Hip flexion | Both | One strength slot + one endurance slot per week **[I]** |
| Single-leg strength | Session A | 2 exercises per block **[W]** |
| Reactive | Session B, early, fresh | Gated — see below **[W]** |

**Tier 2 — alternate fortnightly.** Useful insurance, weaker performance evidence.

| Capacity | Week 1 | Week 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Lateral / adductor | ✅ | |
| Posterior chain accessory | | ✅ |
| Trunk / anti-rotation | superset every week, small | |
| Upper push | ✅ | ✅ |
| Upper pull | ✅ | ✅ |

Upper body stays weekly but at minimum dose: one push, one pull. **[I]** Its value is posture
under fatigue, arm drive over 24 hours, health and enjoyment — not running economy.

**Tier 3 — warm-up tax.** Done inside the warm-up, not given a slot.
Foot and ankle (tibialis raises, short-foot, single-leg balance), low-level pogos and skipping
once cleared, A-skips.

### Session shape

| Block | Time |
|---|---|
| Warm-up — dynamic, plus Tier 3 foot/ankle and low pogos | 10 min |
| Reactive (Session B only, when cleared) | 5–10 min |
| Anchor lift — straight sets | 15–20 min |
| Supersets — pair non-competing movements | 25–35 min |
| Cool-down — mobility and stretching | 5–10 min |

Good pairings: calf + upper push; hip flexion + upper pull; single-leg + trunk; Copenhagen +
row. Supersets save time without costing adaptation **[W]**. Keep anchors as straight sets.

### Pools

Pick from these each block. Rotate between blocks; hold choices stable within one.
Systematic variation helps; random variation hurts **[W]**.

**Hip flexion** — *strength:* standing cable or band hip flexion (3 × 8–12), hanging knee
raise, hanging leg raise, high-knee step-up. *Endurance:* banded or weighted march for time,
isometric knee-drive hold, A-skips.

**Single-leg** — pick 2 per block: Bulgarian split squat, step-up, reverse lunge, walking lunge,
skater squat, single-leg RDL. *Don't run near-duplicates together.*

**Lateral / adductor** — Copenhagen plank (short lever, progress to long), lateral lunge,
Cossack squat, lateral band walk.

**Posterior chain** — pick 2 per block: Nordic curl, Romanian deadlift, hip thrust, back
extension, kettlebell swing. Nordic and RDL are highest value.

**Trunk** — Pallof press, dead bug, side plank, ab wheel, bird dog, landmine rotation,
cable chop.

**Upper push** — bench, dumbbell press, overhead press, landmine press, push-up variations.
**Upper pull** — pull-up, lat pulldown, barbell row. *Flat gym:* single-arm dumbbell row,
inverted row, dead hang.

**Carries** — two only: farmer's (grip + trunk) and suitcase (anti-lateral flexion).

**Foot and ankle** *(warm-up)* — tibialis raises, short-foot holds, single-leg balance,
heel and toe walks.

### Reactive progression (gated)

Plyometrics are high-yield and the main risk to the Achilles. He has to earn each level.
Progress only after two good sessions at the current one. **[W, small study]**

*Corrected Sep 2026 — the previous version of this table used daily-life pain to gate every
level. That was wrong. Per the source (Sancho et al. 2019), daily-life pain ≤2/10 is a single
gate before entering the progression at all; once in it, movement between Levels 1–4 is gated
by pain **from that level's own exercise**, not daily-life pain.*

**Entry gate, before Level 1:** daily-life pain ≤2/10. Below that, no loading progression at
all — not even isometrics.

| Level | Where he is | Work |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Entry gate met; this level's exercise still causing pain **>3/10** | Isometric seated calf holds, up to 3×/day, 5×45s |
| 2 | Level 1 exercise pain has settled to **<3/10**, tolerated | Standing and seated calf raises, hip abduction/extension. Pogos/skipping in warm-up |
| 3 | Level 2 solid, 20 double-leg jumps tolerated at <3/10 | Double-leg jumps, stiff-knee jumps, box jumps |
| 4 | Level 3 solid, 10 single-leg hops tolerated, **seated calf raise ≥1× bodyweight** (`tests.md`) | Single-leg hops, bounding, lateral bounds |
| 5 | Level 4 solid for several weeks | Depth drops |

The source flags **seated calf raise <1.0× bodyweight** as a specific calf-injury risk marker —
in the underlying study, the one participant who tore a calf was the only Level-4 case below
bodyweight, at 0.95× BW. Don't round that up.

The 3/10 hopping-pain threshold is more conservative than the 5/10 allowed for loading
generally (§7) — deliberately, because it's plyometric load on the same tendon.
Both thresholds from `references/evidence-gym-pool.md` **[W]**.

Start around 40–60 low-amplitude contacts per session. Increase hop speed before height or
volume. Do reactive work fresh, early in the session, and keep it 48–72 hours (collagen
synthesis peaks ~37–78h post-loading) from hard running and heavy calf work.

### Blocks

4–6 weeks, each with a stated emphasis.

- **Foundations** (weeks 0–6) — learn the new movements, calf and hip flexion capacity,
  Reactive Level 1–2.
- **Strength** (~6–12 weeks) — heavier anchors, loaded hip flexion, Reactive Level 3 if earned.
- **Power** (~12–18 weeks) — Reactive Level 4 if earned, lighter faster lifting.
- **Durability** (ongoing, and into backyard prep) — higher reps, time under tension,
  hip flexion endurance, carries.

Each block can include one genuinely new lift. Learning counts as progress.

### Maintenance floor

Busy week or race build: one session. Squat or deadlift, calf, hip flexion. Nothing else is
required. Never zero.

### Don't expect too much from any single fix

Ultra fatigue at hour 19+ is mostly central (neural) rather than one muscle giving out **[W]**.
Training hip flexors is sensible and overdue. It won't on its own solve hour 19 — faster laps,
broad durability and back-to-back long days matter as much.

## 7. Achilles rules

- Pain during loading **acceptable up to about 5/10**.
- Must **settle to baseline by next morning**.
- Must not **climb week on week**.
- **Morning first-steps stiffness is the signal.**
- He does not need to stop running. He needs to regulate load.

**If morning symptoms rise two days running:** hold volume, drop the next quality session,
keep the calf work. The calf work is the treatment, not the cause.

## 8. Warm-up and cool-down

**Before a run:** 5 minutes easy, then drills (leg swings, A-skips, high knees) before quality.
**Before gym:** 8–10 minutes dynamic hip and ankle mobility, activation, ramp sets.
**After everything:** 5–10 minutes. Hip flexors, calves (bent and straight knee), hamstrings,
glutes, adductors.

*Evidence note: dynamic warm-ups reduce injury **[W]**. Routine static stretching doesn't,
and long holds (>60s) before a session blunt strength and economy **[W]**. So stretching goes
in the cool-down, and any pre-session holds stay under 60 seconds. It stays because it's part
of what fixed his knee, it takes ten minutes, and he'll actually do it.*

## 9. Phases

**Phase A — Routine and capacity (~12 weeks)**
*Entry state belongs to the active block in `wiki/blocks/`, not here. This page holds the
shape of a phase; the block holds where he actually is.*
- Build to 4–5 runs. Volume 20 → 40km.
- All easy, plus strides from week 3. No threshold for ~6 weeks.
- Gym twice weekly from week one, full coverage grid, loads conservative while learning
  new movements.
- Threshold returns around week 6, volume held flat that fortnight.

**Phase B — Base (≈3 → 9 months)**
- Volume 45 → 65km/week, as the Achilles tolerates.
- Weekly threshold; occasional speed session.
- Long run toward 2.5–3 hours, varied terrain.
- Reactive work progresses.
- Back-to-back long days introduced.

**Phase C — Backyard specific (≈12 weeks out)**
- Lap rehearsal: target lap pace on the hour, 3–6 laps.
- Restart drill: from seated to running stride, repeatedly, under fatigue.
- Transition routine: eat / sit / feet / restart, rehearsed until automatic.
- Foot management as a tested system.
- Back-to-back long days at lap pace.

## 10. Race mode (marathon, dormant)

Triggered only when a marathon is booked.
- Enjoyment constraint temporarily renegotiated, **with his explicit say-so**.
- Volume rises; long runs add marathon-pace segments. The conversion problem (§2) is the
  specific target.
- Strength to maintenance, never zero.
- Taper 2.5–3 weeks: cut volume 40–60%, hold intensity and frequency.
- Entry condition: tolerating base volume pain-free.

---

## 11. Race-day model (backyard)

- **Target lap pace 6:45–7:00/km.** 45–47 min laps, 13–15 min recovery.
- Use the recovery. Keep hips moving before the bell; don't go from seated to running cold.
- Feet are a managed system. Planned changes.
- Fuelling and sleep are solved. Don't over-engineer them.

## 12. Testing

"Better all-round athlete" needs to be measurable, or it becomes a feeling. Retest at the end
of each block. Track the trend, not single results.

**Protocols and results live in `wiki/tests.md`.** The table below is the what; that page is
the how, and it holds every result. Baseline is not yet done (§13).

| Capacity | Test |
|---|---|
| Aerobic | Easy-run pace at a fixed heart rate |
| Threshold | Pace and HR on a standard threshold session (e.g. 3×10min) |
| Speed | Parkrun or 5k time trial, once or twice a block |
| Strength | Estimated 1RM squat and deadlift from working sets |
| Calf | Single-leg heel raise to fatigue, both sides — reps, height, symmetry. Seated calf raise vs bodyweight |
| Hip flexion | Hanging knee raise max reps, or knee-drive hold time |
| Reactive | Single-leg hop distance, both sides |
| Balance | Single-leg stance, eyes closed, both sides |
| Mobility | Couch stretch and ankle knee-to-wall, both sides |
| Behaviour | Sessions where he exceeded the ceiling |
